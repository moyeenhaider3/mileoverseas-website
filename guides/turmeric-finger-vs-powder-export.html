India's Turmeric Heritage

India is the world's largest producer, consumer, and exporter of turmeric, accounting for approximately 80% of global production. Known as "Indian Saffron" for its brilliant golden-yellow color, turmeric (Curcuma longa) has been cultivated in India for over 4,000 years.

Indian Turmeric at a Glance:
  • Scientific Name: Curcuma longa L.
  • Major Growing States: Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Kerala, Meghalaya
  • Harvest Season: June to September (monsoon crop; also January-March in some regions)
  • HS Code: 0910.30 (whole/finger), 0910.11 (ground)
  • Curcumin Content: 3-5% (premium), up to 7%+ (Lakadong variety)
  • Annual Export Volume: 170,000+ MT

The global turmeric market is experiencing significant growth driven by increasing demand for natural anti-inflammatory supplements, clean-label food ingredients, and natural coloring agents. India's diverse agro-climatic zones produce multiple turmeric varieties, each with unique characteristics suited to different market applications.

Buyer's Recommendation: For most B2B importers, turmeric fingers are the better choice. You can visually inspect quality, verify there's no adulteration, and grind at your facility for the freshest product. Powder is ideal only if you're a repackager or endproduct manufacturer needing ready-to-use turmeric.

Indian Turmeric Varieties

India cultivates over 30 turmeric varieties. Here are the most important ones for export:

Nizamabad (Telangana)

Renowned for its exceptional curcumin content (4-5%) and distinctive pungent flavor profile. Nizamabad turmeric is the standard for premium spice brands worldwide. The deep golden-orange color makes it ideal for both culinary and supplement applications.

Salem (Tamil Nadu)

Celebrated for its extraordinarily bright yellow-orange color and complex earthy aroma. Salem turmeric is the variety of choice for food coloring applications and retail spice brands where visual appeal is paramount. Curcumin content: 3-4%.

Rajapuri (Maharashtra)

Prized for its unique sweet-pungent flavor balance that creates signature notes in premium spice blends. Rajapuri features large, plump rhizomes with good curcumin content (3-4%). Popular in Middle Eastern and African markets.

Alleppey (Kerala)

Known internationally, especially in Western markets. Alleppey finger turmeric has a distinctive bright yellow color and curcumin content of 4-5%. It's the benchmark variety for the US and European spice trade. ASTA color value: 200+.

Erode (Tamil Nadu)

Named after the "Turmeric City" of Erode, which houses one of India's largest turmeric trading markets. Erode turmeric has a bright color with curcumin content of 3-4%. It's widely used in food processing and cosmetics.

Lakadong (Meghalaya)

A rare, exceptional variety from India's northeast with extraordinarily high curcumin content of 7-9% — roughly double the standard. Lakadong turmeric commands ultra-premium prices and is primarily used in nutraceutical and pharmaceutical applications.

Understanding Curcumin Content

Curcumin (diferuloylmethane) is the primary bioactive compound in turmeric that gives it the golden-yellow color and most of its health benefits. It's the single most important quality parameter for turmeric in international trade.

Why Curcumin Content Matters

  • Color intensity: Higher curcumin = deeper, more vibrant yellow-orange color
  • Anti-inflammatory properties: Curcumin is a proven anti-inflammatory agent used in supplements worldwide
  • Antioxidant capacity: Directly correlates with curcumin concentration
  • Market value: Every 1% increase in curcumin content can increase the price by 15-25%
  • Regulatory compliance: Some markets have minimum curcumin requirements for "turmeric" labeling

Processing: Single vs Double Polish

Turmeric fingers undergo a polishing process after drying to improve appearance. Understanding polish types helps importers select the right product:

Single Polish

  • Turmeric fingers are polished once in a drum to remove scales and rough outer skin
  • Retains some of the natural outer layer
  • More "rustic" appearance with visible surface texture
  • Lower cost, suitable for industrial grinding
  • Preferred for bulk commodity trade

Double Polish

  • Fingers undergo two rounds of polishing for a smoother, cleaner finish
  • Bright, uniform golden-yellow surface
  • More visually appealing — ideal for retail display and premium markets
  • Higher cost due to additional processing
  • Preferred by premium retail brands and Western markets
Note: Some suppliers apply turmeric powder coating during polishing to enhance color. Mile Overseas supplies only naturally polished turmeric without artificial color enhancement. Always request a "no coating" specification from your supplier.

End-Use Applications

Understanding where your turmeric will be used helps in selecting the right variety and grade:

Culinary / Food Service

Turmeric is essential in curries, rice dishes, marinades, sauces, and seasoning blends. For culinary applications, prioritize vibrant color (Salem, Erode varieties) and balanced flavor (Rajapuri). Standard to premium curcumin grades (2-4%) are sufficient.

Natural Food Coloring

Increasing demand for turmeric as a natural yellow colorant (E100) to replace synthetic tartrazine. High color value (ASTA 200+) is critical. Alleppey and Salem varieties are preferred. Turmeric oleoresin is also extracted for this purpose.

Health Supplements / Nutraceuticals

The fastest-growing segment. Curcumin extracted from turmeric is used in capsules, tablets, and functional beverages. Lakadong and Nizamabad varieties are ideal due to their high curcumin content (5-9%). Organic certification adds significant value.

Cosmetics & Personal Care

Turmeric is used in face masks, creams, soaps, and skincare products. Curcumin's antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties are valued. Fine powder form is preferred, with emphasis on low heavy metal content.

Pharmaceutical

Curcumin is researched extensively for anti-cancer, anti-diabetic, and neuroprotective applications. Pharmaceutical-grade turmeric requires highest purity, documented curcuminoid profile, and compliance with pharmacopoeia standards.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between turmeric finger and turmeric powder for export?

Turmeric fingers are whole dried rhizomes (2-4 inches) with longer shelf life (2-3 years) and higher curcumin retention. Powder is ground turmeric with immediate usability but shorter shelf life and higher adulteration risk. Most B2B importers prefer fingers — you can visually verify quality and grind at destination for the freshest product.

What is the curcumin content of Indian turmeric?

Premium Indian turmeric contains 3-5% curcumin, well above the global average of 2-3%. The Nizamabad and Alleppey varieties offer 4-5%, while the rare Lakadong variety from Meghalaya reaches 7-9%. Higher curcumin = deeper color, stronger health benefits, and higher market value.

What are the major turmeric varieties exported from India?

Key varieties: Nizamabad (high curcumin, pungent), Salem (bright color, earthy), Rajapuri (sweet-pungent, Maharashtra), Alleppey (Western market standard, high curcumin), Erode (bright color, food processing), and Lakadong (ultra-high curcumin 7%+, nutraceuticals).

What is the HS code for turmeric?

Whole/finger turmeric: 0910.30. Ground turmeric powder: 0910.11. These codes apply internationally under the Harmonized System classification.
